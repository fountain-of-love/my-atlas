# Formation

This page describes how education, work, entrepreneurship, and repeated
contexts may have accumulated into Yves's present identity and capabilities.
It is a developmental interpretation, not a replacement for source evidence.

## Two lineages

The repository now keeps two kinds of lineage distinct:

```text
source lineage:
source → extraction → evidence → context → claim

developmental lineage:
context → formative contribution → tension or reinforcement → capability → identity
```

The first asks where a claim came from. The second asks how a context may have
contributed to who Yves is today. A source can support the first lineage
without being enough to establish the second.

## Formative ledger

The weights below are qualitative hypotheses supplied by Yves's account. They
are not objective scores.

| Context | Formative contribution | Weight | Decay | Later reinforcement |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| KU Leuven university education | Theory, analytical formation, and a broad foundation | high / very high | slow | architecture, AI, data, systems thinking |
| Graduate Group T / programming education | Hands-on programming and contact with implementation | high | slow for practice; faster for specific tools | software work, delivery, architecture |
| Hands-on and professional courses | Local techniques and exposure to practical tools | low to medium | often fast for tools; slower for learned habits | data, security, BI, programming |
| Small entrepreneurship | Ownership, autonomy, risk, and consequence | high | slow | later company and architecture decisions |
| SME work | Proximity, versatility, and direct responsibility | medium to high | slow | integration and delivery |
| Corporate work | Scale, governance, coordination, and responsibility for another organisation | high | slow | enterprise architecture and systems stewardship |

The current enterprise-architect identity is treated as an integration point,
not as a source event in the same sense as a diploma or course.

## Developmental sequence

Yves describes a movement across different learning and working conditions:

```text
university theory
    ↕
hands-on programming
    ↓
entrepreneurial ownership and risk
    ↓
SME proximity and versatility
    ↓
corporate scale and governance
    ↓
enterprise architecture as integration
```

This is not intended as a strictly dated chronology. It is a model of
contrasting conditions that were repeatedly encountered and carried forward.

## Present identity hypothesis

Yves's enterprise-architect identity may be understood as the ability to move
between abstraction and implementation, personal ownership and organisational
responsibility, and local action and systemic scale. The university foundation
matters because it provided conceptual depth; programming matters because it
kept that depth in contact with what can actually be built; entrepreneurship,
SME work, and corporate work added consequence, context, and scale.

This is stronger than saying that a particular diploma caused a particular
capability. It describes a cumulative formation in which different contexts
corrected, challenged, and reinforced one another.

## Confidence and open questions

- The overall tension-based account is subject-supplied and meaningful, but
  still requires connection to dated experiences and outcomes.
- Exact chronology, the historical details of the education, and the relative
  weight of each work context remain open.
- The next useful evidence would be specific architecture decisions, products,
  failures, recoveries, and reflections showing how each context changed Yves's
  way of working.
