# Tensions

These are not problems to eliminate. They are productive fields in which Yves
describes learning, judgement, and capability as having developed.

## Theory and practice

### Poles

- University education: concepts, theory, models, and analytical reasoning.
- Graduate programming and hands-on work: implementation, debugging, and
  contact with technical reality.

### Capability potentially emerging from the field

Architecture that remains conceptually coherent while staying implementable.
The practical test is not whether theory is discarded, but whether it survives
contact with systems that must actually work.

### Supporting contexts

- [Commercial Engineer — KU Leuven](../contexts/education/commercial-engineer-kuleuven.md)
- [Graduate Programming — UCLL](../contexts/education/graduate-programming-ucll.md)
- [Software engineering and secure development](../contexts/learning/software-engineering-and-secure-development.md)

## Builder and architect

### Poles

- Building and changing concrete software.
- Designing boundaries, structures, and systems from a higher level.

### Capability potentially emerging from the field

Translating between implementation detail and system-level design, with enough
contact with both sides to recognise when an architecture is elegant but
unbuildable.

## Autonomy and organisational responsibility

### Poles

- Entrepreneurship: direct ownership, personal risk, and consequence.
- SME and corporate work: responsibility exercised within structures created
  with and for other people.

### Capability potentially emerging from the field

Making decisions with both agency and stewardship in view: taking initiative
without forgetting the people, systems, and obligations affected by the
decision.

## Proximity and scale

### Poles

- Small companies and SMEs: closeness to people, problems, and consequences.
- Corporate settings: scale, coordination, governance, and institutional
  complexity.

### Capability potentially emerging from the field

Carrying local reality into larger systems without losing sight of the people
and work that the system is meant to serve.

## Breadth and depth

### Poles

- Cross-disciplinary exposure: business, biotechnology, human-machine
  communication, programming, data, and AI.
- The need to develop sufficient depth to make reliable technical and
  architectural decisions.

### Capability potentially emerging from the field

Integration across domains, together with discernment about where deeper
specialisation is required. Breadth is treated as a resource and a tension,
not automatically as an advantage.

## Capability map

The candidate capabilities emerging from these fields are deliberately
phrased as hypotheses:

- architecture that connects theory and implementation;
- systems thinking grounded in organisational reality;
- adaptive movement between autonomy and stewardship;
- integration across technical and human domains;
- judgement about scale, consequence, and necessary depth.

These should only become established capabilities when supported by repeated
contexts, concrete outcomes, and Yves's reflection.
