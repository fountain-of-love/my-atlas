
# Self-model

The self-model describes a person at the person level. It gives a natural sequence:

**Experience → Identity → Interaction → Growth → Behaviour**

The sequence is a lens for understanding a person, not a claim that people develop through five rigid stages. Each word answers a different question:

- **Experience**: What has shaped you?
- **Identity**: Who are you?
- **Interaction**: How do you relate?
- **Growth**: Where are you developing toward?
- **Behaviour**: How do you actually show up and act?

Together, the five lenses move from experience, through identity and interaction, toward growth and behaviour. They should be read as connected aspects of one person rather than as isolated categories.

## Reading the five lenses

The model can be held through five plain-language questions:

| Lens | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Experience** | What has shaped the person? |
| **Identity** | Who is the person, and what do they give form to? |
| **Interaction** | How does the person relate and move with others? |
| **Growth** | What direction and longer movement organise the person? |
| **Behaviour** | How does the person make things happen? |

The lenses are deliberately broad. They can hold both inner and observable aspects of a person, while keeping the focus on the person rather than on any particular representation, platform, research method, or operational system.

| Personal lens   | Person in general                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Experience**  | **What has shaped the person:** history, experiences, track record, prior choices, learned patterns, formative contexts, accumulated knowledge, recurring lessons.                                    |
| **Identity**    | **Who the person has become and gives form to:** identity, values, worldview, roles, principles, style, expression, positioning, and the forms through which they present themselves.                 |
| **Interaction** | **How the person relates and moves with others:** collaboration, communication, relationships, conflict, negotiation, learning, exchange, social patterns, and ways of connecting different contexts. |
| **Growth**      | **What organises the person's direction:** intentions, priorities, ambitions, commitments, goals, desired future, longer trajectories, and the choices that organise movement toward them.            |
| **Behaviour**   | **How the person makes things happen:** capabilities, skills, habits, practices, ways of working, tools, roles, behaviours, and capacity to turn intention into action.                               |

## Boundary and interpretation

This is the person-level model. It is not a model of an online presence, a profile-construction workflow, or a system used to research and represent someone. Those may later be treated as separate applications or observational mirrors of this model, but they must not redefine its five lenses. When the model is populated, keep a clear distinction between:

- what the person explicitly states or demonstrates;
- what can reasonably be inferred from recurring patterns; and
- what remains unknown or should not be presumed.

In particular, **Growth** should not be reduced to visible goals or output: it includes the direction, priorities, commitments, and longer trajectories that organise movement. Likewise, **Identity** is broader than public expression: expression is one way identity gives form, not its complete definition.

The model observes or describes five dimensions of the person, but no single source or manifestation is assumed to capture the whole person.

## Person-level clarification of the Enigma mapping

The Enigma terms describe canonical functions. The person lenses describe what those functions mean when applied to a person. They are related mappings, not interchangeable synonyms:

- **Inference → Experience:** Inference concerns sense-making; at the person level, Experience is what has shaped the person, including the history and accumulated learning from which sense-making develops.
- **Design → Identity:** Design concerns giving form; at the person level, Identity is broader than expression and includes values, worldview, roles, principles, and positioning. Design describes how identity takes form.
- **Coordination → Interaction:** Coordination concerns how parts move together; at the person level, Interaction includes relationships, communication, collaboration, conflict, exchange, and movement between contexts.
- **Orchestration → Growth:** Orchestration concerns what organises direction; at the person level, Growth includes intentions, priorities, commitments, ambitions, and longer trajectories.
- **Execution → Behaviour:** Execution concerns making something real; at the person level, Behaviour includes the capabilities, skills, habits, practices, tools, and ways of working through which action becomes possible.

This preserves the precision of the canonical model while allowing the self-model to speak in natural person-level terms.

## Relation with Enigma

Enigma is the canonical worldview model applied across this repository and, where relevant, across other repositories. It provides a shared structural and functional language for relating different domains to the same underlying patterns. The self-model uses that language to connect person-level lenses with Enigma's canonical elements without replacing the person-level meaning of those lenses.

| Canonical model | Functional model | Person lens     | Core meaning                                         |
| --------------- | ---------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Inference       | Input            | **Experience**  | What shaped me?                                      |
| Design          | Output           | **Identity**    | Who am I becoming / expressing?                      |
| Coordination    | Process          | **Interaction** | How do I relate and move with others?                |
| Orchestration   | Program          | **Growth**      | Where am I heading and what organises that movement? |
| Execution       | Workers          | **Behaviour**   | How do I act and make things real?                   |
