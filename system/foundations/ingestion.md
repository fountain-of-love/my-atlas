# Ingestion

Ingestion is the movement from existing material into the person-first self-model. It is not document conversion. It is a process of observing, extracting, testing, and integrating signal.

## Position in the Atlas

`Input → Observation → Candidate source entry → Validation → Source → Output`

The input remains an input. The source entry becomes the canonical, human-readable model. An output is a later projection for a particular audience or purpose.

## Processing loop

### 1. Register

Give the input an ID, preserve its location, note its kind, period, audience, and provenance, and mark its processing status.

### 2. Read for signal

Read the input as evidence about the person, not as a form to be copied. Mark passages or facts that reveal:

- identity or self-understanding;
- recurring patterns;
- capabilities demonstrated in context;
- ways of working;
- values, beliefs, or philosophy;
- interests and direction;
- experiences, projects, relationships, and outcomes;
- evidence, uncertainty, contradiction, or change over time.

### 3. Separate observation from interpretation

Record what the source actually says or shows before proposing what it may mean. Keep at least these distinctions visible:

- **Source observation** — what is present in the input;
- **Claim** — a statement the Atlas might make;
- **Interpretation** — a proposed meaning or pattern;
- **Evidence** — what supports or qualifies the claim;
- **Uncertainty** — what remains unresolved or source-dependent.

### 4. Cluster against the fishbone

Place observations against the person-first source and its contextual branches. Do not force an observation into the input’s original categories. A CV section may reveal a way of working; a book passage may reveal a capability; a project may reveal a value.

### 5. Draft candidate entries

Create small, provisional entries only where a recurring or useful signal is strong enough. Link each candidate to its input and relevant evidence. Prefer a few rich candidates over a comprehensive rewrite.

### 6. Validate with Yves

Yves validates resonance, truth, nuance, boundaries, and what should remain private. Enigma may compare sources, expose contradictions, suggest alternatives, and identify blind spots, but does not silently settle personal meaning.

### 7. Integrate

Move validated material into `source/`. Connect it to contexts, evidence, relationships, and the shared dictionary. Record uncertainty instead of smoothing it away.

### 8. Reflect and feed back

Record what the input changed, confirmed, complicated, or failed to reveal. Update the source inventory and refine the relevant specification only when the ingestion exposes a recurring need.

## Source comparison

Different sources are different perspectives, not automatically competing versions. Compare them for:

- continuity — what remains stable;
- development — what has changed or deepened;
- selection — what is emphasized for a particular audience;
- omission — what disappears in a particular expression;
- tension — where sources disagree or reveal different selves in context.

An older broad CV, a recent tailored CV, online presence, and book writing should therefore be read as complementary lenses.

## First-pass limit

The first pass should aim to produce a useful signal, not a complete biography. A good initial cycle might yield three candidate patterns, three capabilities, two ways of working, two values or beliefs, and several linked contexts or evidence items.

The loop can be repeated with the next source. Completeness is allowed to emerge from comparison.
